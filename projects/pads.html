PADS: Police Array Drone System

An autonomous multi-drone system for responding to burglaries in Westminster.

Overview

PADS was our third-year group design project (May 2026), with Kaito Bremner, Alrik Cheung and Vikyrthan Kirushnamoorthy. Police on the ground often reach a burglary after the suspects have fled, so PADS sends drones that reach a reported crime in under 2 minutes and keep the suspects in view until officers arrive.

Small Hummingbird quadcopters pursue suspects at low altitude, an Eagle VTOL drone relays their links over the urban canyon, and docks and a ground station handle charging, perception in the cloud, and the operators. I was responsible for station placement and dispatch, and for path planning, control, state estimation and multi-drone coordination.

Station placement

Using about 17,000 burglaries recorded in Westminster from 2023 to 2025, I placed drone stations with a weighted k-means that favours credible alarms and avoids dense housing. Five stations first meet the 2-minute target at the 95th percentile, and 10 stations give a P95 response of 90 s and a mean of 69 s.

Path planning and control

A* plans the high-altitude transit to the incident, and an MPC tracks the suspect at low altitude. I reduced the MPC to 2D so it meets the 20 Hz real-time target on a Jetson Orin NX, tested it on a corridor turn, then on a 120 s chase through Westminster's road network built from OpenStreetMap. Bayesian optimisation then tuned the MPC weights to cut overshoot, jerk and energy.

Depth and state estimation

The Hummingbird is too small for a useful stereo baseline, so I proposed monocular homography to estimate the suspect's range. I modelled the sensor noise and tuned a Kalman filter, which brings drone and target position errors within the 0.5 m and 1 m bounds and sets the minimum sensor accuracy for the hardware and vision teams.

Multi-drone coordination

Three Hummingbirds fly per group of suspects. I compared formation controllers in simulation; all met the collision-avoidance target, and leader-follower gave the lowest formation error. When suspects split up, the Hungarian algorithm assigns drones to the new groups, skipping drones with low battery.

The rest of the system

Hardware and communications (Alrik Cheung)

Alrik designed the Hummingbird hardware, the Eagle relay and the docks, and the radio links between them. The Hummingbird reaches a thrust-to-weight ratio above 2 and tracks for 9 min 48 s.

Perception and data fusion (Kaito Bremner)

Kaito built the perception system: YOLO detection and BoT-SORT tracking on the drone, with a larger model on the ground server that recovers tracking accuracy, and a hybrid fusion of RGB, NIR, thermal and LiDAR across lighting conditions.

Human-robot interface and command centre (Vikyrthan Kirushnamoorthy)

Vikyrthan built the apps that civilians use to report crimes, by form, chatbot or voice, and the operator interface for controlling drones and formations, with a heads-up display. He also designed the command and control centre.
